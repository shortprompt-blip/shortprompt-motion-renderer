Before you launch GTA Six, change these settings on your PS5. They can make the image clearer, the controls more comfortable, and the game feel more responsive. But one important warning: no menu setting unlocks sixty frames per second. Rockstar says GTA Six targets thirty FPS on console.

First, open PS5 Settings, then Screen and Video. Leave Resolution on Automatic, so your console selects the highest mode your TV supports. If your TV supports HDR, enable it when supported and run Adjust HDR. Calibrate it for your actual display, not a random online number.

Next, enable VRR and 120 Hertz output only if your TV supports them. Turn on ALLM if available, or use your TV's Game Mode. These options can improve compatibility and reduce display processing delay, but they do not turn GTA Six into a 120 FPS game.

Now open GTA Six Settings. Under Display, lower Motion Blur Strength to zero if you want a sharper image while turning or driving. Then calibrate the game's HDR using your screen. Avoid copying brightness values from another television.

Under Camera, switch Camera Shake off if it bothers you, and reduce Camera Bob until movement feels comfortable. Under Controller, try Partial Targeting with Aim Assist around four as a starting point, then adjust it to your skill and preference.

Under Gameplay, choose a movement speed that suits you. Turn subtitles on if you want every line of dialogue. If you stream or record, check the Audio options and mute licensed music when needed.

One last thing: on PS5 Pro, Rockstar says GTA Six uses PSSR for improved clarity and adds ray-traced shadows. Those are platform features, not hidden performance switches.
