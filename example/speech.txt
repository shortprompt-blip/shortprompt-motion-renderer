Gemini 4 Argon is everywhere right now.

But did Google actually build the best AI model?

Let's compare the numbers.

On DeepSWE version 1.1, Google reports Argon at 77.9 percent. Claude Opus 5.5 is at 74.2. GPT-6 Astra is at 74.1. Argon wins this one.

But change the benchmark.

On FrontierSWE version 2, Argon drops to 55 percent. GPT-6 Astra reaches 65.5. Claude Opus 5.5 reaches 62.3.

So on this coding benchmark, Argon is last.

Terminal-Bench 4.0 tells a similar story. Argon scores 57.4 percent. Opus 5.5 scores 66.4. That's a nine-point gap in Claude's favor.

So the claim that Gemini 4 Argon simply beats every other model at coding is not supported by the published numbers.

But there is another side.

On long-context reasoning, Argon looks extremely strong. Google reports 84.2 percent on GraphWalks for problems from 256 thousand to one million tokens.

On LVBench, which measures long-video understanding, Argon reaches 91.7 percent.

And on AutomationBench, Google reports 51.3 percent for Argon, ahead of GPT-6 Astra at 41.4 and Claude Opus 5.5 at 42.5.

Cybersecurity is another major strength.

On CWE-bench version 1, Argon scores 68 percent, tied for the top published score in the comparison.

So what is Gemini 4 Argon actually best at?

Not everything.

The pattern is much more interesting.

Argon looks particularly strong when the job requires long reasoning, huge inputs, knowledge work, multimodal understanding, and complex workflows.

But terminal-heavy agent work is a different story.

And there is one more caveat.

These comparisons come from Google's launch evaluation table. Argon is only beginning its limited rollout, so independent reproduction is still important.

The takeaway?

Gemini 4 Argon is absolutely a frontier model.

But the real story isn't 'Google destroyed everyone.'

It's that the AI race is becoming benchmark-specific.

The best model depends on what you actually ask it to do.
