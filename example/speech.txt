[03 | 060.79-094.46s | PREPARE]
Before opening the creator, set up the recording. Use a phone or webcam at eye level, keep the lens close to your natural eye line, and frame your head and upper body with a little space around you. Choose a quiet room with even light on your face. Avoid strong backlighting, busy backgrounds, and rapid camera movement. Wear something simple, and record in a way that feels like your normal on-camera delivery.

[04 | 094.46-126.73s | RECORD]
For the Avatar V real-person flow, HeyGen’s current help guide asks for a short fifteen-second video. Treat that as a reference capture, not a full tutorial recording. Look into the lens, speak naturally, and include clear but relaxed facial expressions and gestures. Keep your face visible and avoid cuts or filters. The source clip should represent you accurately; don’t upload another person’s likeness unless you have their explicit permission.

[05 | 126.73-159.46s | CREATE]
Open HeyGen and go to Avatar, then Avatars. Choose New Avatar and the option to clone a real person, where available. Follow the current capture or upload prompts and submit the reference clip. The official guide describes Avatar V as learning the person’s movement and expressiveness from the video. The processing time and available model options can differ, so wait for the avatar to appear in your library before continuing.

[06 | 159.46-190.79s | CONSENT AND LOOK]
Complete the consent recording when the platform asks for it. This is an identity and permission step, not something to skip. Once the avatar is ready, review the available looks. Choose a clear, front-facing image with a natural expression and suitable lighting. HeyGen’s look tools can vary clothing, background, and camera angle, but consistency matters more than novelty. Start with one clean presenter look before creating variations.

[08 | 216.04-251.58s | SCRIPT AND VOICE]
Select the avatar and choose Use in video to open the video editor, or use the current quick creation flow if it is available in your account. Add a short script first—around twenty to thirty seconds is enough for a useful test. Choose a voice you’re authorized to use, or create your own voice clone by completing its consent process. Read the script aloud once before generating; awkward written sentences usually sound awkward when spoken.

[09 | 251.58-284.32s | GENERATE]
Generate the first version and review it from beginning to end. Check lip synchronization, pronunciation of names, facial movement, and whether the gestures fit the words. Don’t judge only the preview frame. If a sentence looks unnatural, shorten it and regenerate that section. For a Short, keep one idea per video and make the opening sentence deliver the promise immediately. A realistic avatar still needs a strong script and editing.

Add captions, but keep them readable on a phone and away from the platform controls. Use cuts to remove pauses, and keep background music low enough that every word remains clear. [11 | 317.99-354.46s | LIMITS AND PRIVACY]
A digital twin can save repeated recording, but it is not a substitute for editorial judgment. Review every output before publishing. Check the current plan and credit rules because generation costs and model availability change. Keep source footage and voice data private, use only your own likeness or licensed consent, and never use an avatar to impersonate someone or mislead viewers. For important claims, show real evidence rather than asking the avatar to make the video feel authoritative.
