Want to make an AI video without paying for three different tools? Here is a practical pipeline: prompt, video, then voice.

First, write the idea in Google AI Studio. Ask for a short script and one clear visual prompt. Be specific: subject, action, setting, camera movement, lighting, and aspect ratio. Keep the clip simple. One shot is easier to generate than a whole movie.

Here is a reusable prompt: “Create a five-second cinematic shot of a tiny robot walking through a rainy neon city at night. Slow tracking camera, reflections on the pavement, blue and magenta lighting, no text, sixteen-by-nine landscape.” Copy the final prompt before moving to the video tool.

For the video step, Luma Dream Machine has a free tier, but read the catch: its official plan guide describes watermarked draft output and personal-use limits. That means it is useful for learning and testing, but do not assume the free result is cleared for a monetized or commercial video. Check the current plan before you generate; quotas and model access can change.

Generate a short clip, review it, and save the result. If the free tier does not fit your publishing rights, use footage you own or assets with a license that covers your project instead. A free button does not automatically mean commercial permission.

Now create the voiceover. Paste your approved script into TTSMaker, choose a voice and language, preview it, then download the audio. Its current site lists a twenty-thousand-character weekly quota, with some voices excluded from the quota, and its licensing page says generated audio can be used commercially. Check the terms again before publishing.

Finally, assemble the clip and voice in a free editor. Match the voice to the visuals, add captions, and keep music low enough that every word stays clear.

The workflow is simple: AI Studio for the script, a video generator for the shot, TTSMaker for narration, and an editor for the final cut. Free tiers have limits, so verify credits and usage rights first.
