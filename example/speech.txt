Google just unveiled Gemini 4 Argon — and this is not another small model update.

Argon is Google's new frontier model for complex coding, enterprise knowledge work, and cybersecurity.

But the number getting the most attention is one million.

Google says Argon's output limit jumps from 64 thousand tokens to an industry-leading one million tokens.

That means a single trajectory can contain hundreds of thousands of generated tokens for a long, complex task.

And Google is showing why that matters.

Its engineers are using Argon for large code migrations, including projects scaling to more than 800 thousand lines of code.

In one example, Argon helped replace 32 thousand lines of SIMD code in a Rust video decoder, producing a version that Google says runs 2.7 times faster than the existing Rust port.

On DeepSWE version 1.1, Google's reported score is 77.9 percent for real-world, long-horizon software engineering tasks.

Then there is cybersecurity.

Google says Argon can autonomously find, validate, and patch critical vulnerabilities.

On CWE-bench version 1, Google reports a top score of 68 percent.

And this is where the launch gets unusual.

Argon is not broadly available yet.

It is first rolling out to trusted cyber defenders through Google's Fairwind program, while Google strengthens its frontier safety measures.

The introductory API price is two dollars per million input tokens and ten dollars per million output tokens. After the introductory period, Google says the price will become four dollars and twenty dollars.

So what is Gemini 4 Argon really?

Not simply a chatbot with a bigger context window.

Google is positioning it as a model for long-horizon work — coding, research, enterprise workflows, and defensive cybersecurity.

The real test will be what happens when developers get access.

One million output tokens sounds huge.

Now we have to see what Argon can actually do with them.
