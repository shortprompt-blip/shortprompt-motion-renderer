If your YouTube video gets impressions but not enough clicks, your thumbnail could be part of the problem.

Open YouTube Studio and check your video analytics. Look at impressions and click-through rate together.

A low CTR does not automatically mean your thumbnail is bad. CTR changes with traffic source and audience, and it can fall as YouTube shows your video to a broader audience.

But if impressions are growing while clicks stay weak, check your packaging: thumbnail and title.

Make the thumbnail easier to understand in one second: one clear idea, readable text, strong contrast, and a promise that matches the video.

And if Test & Compare is available, test different thumbnail versions and let the data decide.
