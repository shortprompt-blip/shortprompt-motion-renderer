Want more YouTube subscribers from the views you already get? Stop judging every video by views alone.

Open YouTube Studio and check which videos gained subscribers. Look at subscriber gains alongside views, not instead of them.

Here’s an illustrative comparison. Video A gets forty-two thousand views and eighty-four subscribers. Video B gets eight thousand four hundred views and one hundred twenty-six subscribers.

That works out to two subscribers per thousand views for Video A, versus fifteen for Video B. These are simulated numbers, not official channel data or a YouTube benchmark. The ratio is a simple comparison you can calculate yourself.

Next, inspect audience retention. Find the moments viewers stay for, then identify the problem or payoff that made the video useful.

Turn that insight into a related follow-up video. Connect the videos with a relevant end screen or playlist, and make your call to action specific: tell viewers what useful content they can expect next.

Each week, find the videos that attract subscribers, compare them fairly, study retention, and make the next video that serves the same audience.
