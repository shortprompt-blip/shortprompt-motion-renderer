Gemini 4 is coming. But how much of what you are hearing about it is actually real?

Google has confirmed that Gemini 4 is in development. In July, Google said it had started its most ambitious pre-training run yet for Gemini 4. And in September, DeepMind chief Koray Kavukcuoglu said the model had moved into post-training and could arrive well before the end of 2026.

But Google has not published the final Gemini 4 model card, API specification, benchmark results, or launch configuration.

So let's separate the confirmed facts from the leaks.

The most interesting leak is a reported 256-thousand-token output limit. For comparison, Google's current Gemini 3.8 Flash documentation lists a maximum output of 65,536 tokens. That means the leaked number would be roughly four times larger.

Why does that matter? Because a huge output budget could make long code transformations, large technical reports and extended agentic workflows much more practical.

But there is an important catch: this number comes from reporting about an alleged internal checkpoint. A leaked checkpoint is not the same thing as a shipping product.

Then things get much less certain.

Leak coverage has also discussed a multi-million-token context window and a selectable high-effort reasoning mode. Some reports describe a two-million-token context figure, while other posts make much larger claims.

And this is exactly where we need to slow down.

The most extreme claims — including ten-million-token context, persistent memory and other unusual capabilities — do not have the same level of evidence. They should be treated as rumors, not specifications.

There is another reason Gemini 4 is getting attention.

Anonymous tests on public model-comparison platforms have been attributed by the community to possible Gemini 4 checkpoints. People have reported impressive SVG generation, three-dimensional scenes and interactive simulations.

But attribution is still uncertain.

So those demonstrations are interesting evidence of what a possible checkpoint may be capable of — not proof of what Gemini 4 will ship with.

And Google is not competing against the models that existed six months ago.

GPT-6 Astra is already deployed, with OpenAI positioning it at the frontier for computer use, browsing, software engineering, science and professional work.

Claude Opus 5.5 is also already available. Anthropic describes it as a model for complex work, and reported that one tester completed a six-hundred-eighty-thousand-line code migration in less than a day.

And Google's own Gemini 3.8 Flash already supports more than one million input tokens, thinking, code execution and computer use in preview.

So Gemini 4 does not just need a bigger context window or a bigger output limit.

It needs to prove five things.

Can it reason better? Can it work autonomously for longer? Can it modify and verify large codebases? Can it use a computer reliably? And most importantly, can independent testers reproduce the results?

That is the real benchmark.

Right now, the strongest conclusion is simple.

Gemini 4 is real. Its training is real. The leaked specifications are interesting. Some reported demonstrations are impressive.

But the final capability is still unproven.

So when you see a headline saying Gemini 4 destroys every other model, look for the evidence behind the claim.

Because 256-thousand tokens is a specification.

Useful, reliable autonomy is the real test.
