Gemini 4 is coming. But how much of what you are hearing about it is actually real?

Google has confirmed one important thing: Gemini 4 is being trained. In July, Google described it as its most ambitious pre-training run yet. More recent reporting says the model has moved into refinement. But Google has not published the final model card, API specification, benchmark table, or launch configuration.

So let’s separate the confirmed facts from the leaks.

The most interesting leak is a reported two-hundred-fifty-six-thousand-token output limit. For comparison, Google’s current Gemini 3.8 Flash documentation lists a maximum output of sixty-five-thousand five-hundred thirty-six tokens. If the leaked Gemini 4 number is real, that would be roughly four times larger.

Why does that matter? A larger output budget could make giant code transformations, long technical reports and extended agent workflows more practical. But there is a catch: this is a leak about an alleged checkpoint. A training checkpoint is not the same thing as a shipping product.

Then the claims get much less certain.

Leak coverage has also discussed a multi-million-token context window and a selectable high-effort reasoning mode. A two-million-token figure appears in some reporting, but Google has not published it as an official Gemini 4 specification.

And the most extreme claims, including ten-million-token context, persistent memory and dramatic benchmark numbers, need much stronger evidence. They should be treated as rumors, not specifications.

There is another reason Gemini 4 is getting attention.

Anonymous model-comparison tests have been attributed by the community to possible Gemini 4 checkpoints. Reports describe impressive SVG generation, three-dimensional scenes and interactive simulations. But the attribution is not proven. An anonymous model label does not tell us what model is actually running behind it.

So those demonstrations are interesting clues, not proof of what Gemini 4 will ship with.

And Google is not competing against the models that existed six months ago.

GPT-6 Astra is already shipping. OpenAI positions it for complex reasoning, coding, research, browsing and computer use, with a one-point-zero-five-million-token context window and a one-hundred-twenty-eight-thousand-token maximum output.

Claude Fable 5.1 is also available. Anthropic positions it as a frontier model for coding, knowledge work and long-running problem solving.

And Google’s own Gemini 3.8 Flash already supports more than one million input tokens, sixty-five-thousand five-hundred thirty-six output tokens, thinking and computer use in preview.

So Gemini 4 does not just need a bigger context window or a bigger output limit.

It needs to prove four things.

Can it reason better? Can it work autonomously for longer? Can it modify and verify large codebases? And can it use a computer reliably and finish the task?

That is the real benchmark.

Right now, the strongest conclusion is simple.

Gemini 4 is real. Its training is real. The leaked specifications are interesting. Some reported demonstrations are impressive.

But the final capability is still unproven.

So when you see a headline saying Gemini 4 destroys every other model, look for the evidence behind the claim.

Because two-hundred-fifty-six-thousand tokens is a specification.

Reliable autonomy is the real test.
